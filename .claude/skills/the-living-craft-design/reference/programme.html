01

What this is

The market is filling with courses on how to use AI tools. This is not one. The tools turn over every few months. The judgment about what to ship, what to instrument and what to refuse does not.

Six weeks, about five hours a week, eight people. You bring a system you are responsible for and we take it apart on the two axes that decide whether an agentic system survives production: whether you can tell that it works, and whether someone else can make it do something you did not intend.

I have led 150 engineers across the US, UK, China and India, and the systems I ran served more than 100 million users. Most of what I will teach you came out of the incidents rather than the designs.

Eight seats. I read every application myself, and most get a no.

illustration 600 × 600 · on ember

Admission is by application, not by payment. I am looking for people who own something in production and can say plainly what went wrong with it.

02

Six weeks

Weeks 5 and 6 are not written. They are set in week 2, once I have seen the eight systems in the room.

Week 1

The system you brought

Each of you presents one agentic system you are on the hook for. No slides. The reading list for the cohort is written this week, out of what the eight of you brought.

Week 2

Architecture, and what it costs later

The four or five shapes an agentic system takes, and the bill each one sends you in month nine. We pick the shape for each system in the room and write down what we are giving up.

Week 3

Where the retry loop failed

Two incidents, six weeks apart, one wrapper that looked idempotent. The pattern was right and the deployment around it was not. We read the config, not the design doc.

Week 4

Evaluation you can defend

How you tell that it works, in a form that survives a review. Confidence is reported as a fraction, never a percentage — 87% implies a calibration nobody has.

03

The reviewer reads your pull requests

An agent reads every pull request you open during the six weeks and posts what it found. It marks its own work — what it read, what it could not read, and what it declined to answer.
