## For people who already work with system design

You may be an experienced engineer, architect or engineering leader. You understand that a system’s design involves trade-offs, and you want to deepen how you approach those choices in agentic systems.

Bring prior system-design exposure, a learning goal and the willingness to build, explain your decisions and revise your work through feedback.

## Follow a decision beyond the demo

Imagine a returns assistant that reads a policy and recommends a refund. Now give it a tool that can issue the refund. The system can move money, and its design needs to account for that responsibility.

What evidence permits the action? How does it recognise a repeated request? What happens when the policy is unclear or a tool result is uncertain?

This illustrative example shows the kind of connection you will practise: from a design choice to behaviour you can examine.

## See what a review can change

Imagine a checker rejects an agent's result, and the workflow repeats the same attempt. A useful review asks what new evidence the next attempt has. If nothing changes, the next design question is how to recover or stop.

This is an illustrative teaching example. It shows how a question can lead to a change you can build and examine. The aim is to develop how you reason, alongside what you produce.

## Develop practical design judgment

Build a working agentic system and connect its behaviour to the architecture behind it. Practise explaining why a boundary exists, what evidence supports a decision and what you would change next.

Develop how you design and critique agentic systems, review a team’s proposal and provide useful guidance.

## What the work explores

Purpose and constraints: frame the task, the context and the choices the system needs to support.

Tools and authority: examine what the system may do, the evidence it needs and where a person should decide.

Evaluation: inspect behaviour and failures, and connect findings to a useful design change.

Reliability and cost: consider uncertain results, retries, review effort and the trade-offs around the model.

Design review: explain the architecture, listen to challenges and identify the next useful piece of work.

## Build, examine and revise

The working system gives the discussions something concrete to return to. Build an approach, examine what happens and use continuous feedback to understand strengths, gaps and follow-up work.

Industry-specific practice is the default. Company-specific work needs a separate conversation about scope and permission.
