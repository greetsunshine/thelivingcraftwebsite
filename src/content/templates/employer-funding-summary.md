## How to use it

Fill in your own version of the second section before anything else. Everything on this page
rests on there being a real decision in front of your team; without one, the case is that you
would like to learn something, which is a fair thing to want and a weak thing to fund.

Keep it to one page. Your manager is very likely forwarding it to somebody who will read only
the first section and the last one, so those two must work alone.

Two things to leave out, both tempting. Do not include a salary or market-rate argument — it
reads as leverage and changes the conversation into a different one. And do not quote a fee
from memory: fees, schedule, payment, refund and access terms are confirmed in the final
offer, before you commit to anything, and a number you half-remember is the one thing in the
document your manager can catch you out on.

## If you do not know the figure yet

That is the ordinary case, and the template is built for it. Applying starts a fit
conversation, and the terms arrive with the offer at the end of it. You can send this summary
with the cost section blank and a line saying the figure follows — an approval in principle is
usually what you actually need first.

The [application](/) asks about funding route as part of the fit conversation, so saying
"my employer may fund this" costs nothing and changes nothing about how the application is
read.
